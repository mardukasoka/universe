Stellar Seeds, Remnants & Transitions

From molecular clouds and free-floating planetary-mass objects to white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes. A research register, not a claim that every proposed pathway has been observed.

Select an evidence category to filter the questions. This standalone page makes no network requests.

01 · Stellar formation and companions

ESTABLISHED

Cold molecular-cloud cores collapse and fragment. Rotation, turbulence, magnetic fields and accretion shape protostars and disks. T Tauri and Herbig Ae/Be stars are young stellar classes; Herbig–Haro objects are jet-driven shocks, not stars.

OPEN

How low in mass can direct cloud fragmentation go? How do Orion's candidate Jupiter-mass binary objects (JuMBOs) form, and which candidates are genuinely bound binaries?

ESTABLISHED

Multiple stars form; Castor is a hierarchical six-star system. Proplyds are protoplanetary disks.

OPEN

What controls fragmentation into single, binary and higher-order stellar systems, as opposed to planetary or brown-dwarf companions?

02 · Mass thresholds and fusion

ESTABLISHED

Sustained hydrogen fusion begins near 75–80 Jupiter masses, depending on composition. Deuterium burning is often associated with approximately 13 Jupiter masses, but neither threshold uniquely determines formation history.

ESTABLISHED

Late-M, L, T and Y describe spectral or atmospheric classes, not a sequence of distinct fusion stages. Some massive brown dwarfs can burn lithium.

OPEN

How frequently do disk-born objects accrete across the planet/brown-dwarf or brown-dwarf/star mass thresholds?

03 · Engulfment and surviving planets

ESTABLISHED

Jupiter has a hydrogen-helium envelope, electrically conducting metallic hydrogen and probably a dilute heavy-element core. Magnetic fields do not eliminate tides, radiation or hydrodynamic drag inside stars.

OPEN

Can magnetic interactions modify the stripping of planets engulfed by red giants? Could a heavy-element remnant survive?

ESTABLISHED

Planets are observed around white dwarfs and neutron stars; their existence alone does not establish survival through the progenitor's envelope or explosion.

OPEN

Which pulsar planets survived stellar evolution, and which formed later from fallback or transferred material?

04 · Compact remnants

ESTABLISHED

White dwarfs are electron-degenerate stellar remnants. Novae and micronovae occur on existing accreting white dwarfs. Brown dwarfs cool but do not turn into white dwarfs through cooling.

OPEN

What is the maximum stable neutron-star mass across equations of state and spin rates? Which compact objects near the lower mass gap are neutron stars or black holes?

ESTABLISHED

Neutron-star formation involves gravitational collapse and weak-interaction electron capture. A neutron star can collapse after mass gain or loss of rotational support; Hawking evaporation does not convert stellar-mass black holes back into neutron stars.

OPEN

Could asymmetric isolated neutron-star collapse yield a distinguishable gravitational-wave burst or ringdown?

SPECULATIVE

A distinct mechanism in which spacetime is physically “squeezed out of matter” is not an established theory or observational result.

05 · Nested stars and cosmic futures

ESTABLISHED

Common-envelope evolution can embed a companion within a giant star's envelope and drive inspiral.

OPEN

Do long-lived Thorne–Żytkow objects with neutron-star cores exist? Did early-universe quasi-stars with embedded accreting black holes form? What was the Population III stellar mass distribution?

ESTABLISHED

White dwarfs cool; black dwarfs are predicted ultra-cold remnants, but the universe is too young for ordinary stellar evolution to have produced them.

SPECULATIVE

Intact black dwarfs surviving a previous Big Crunch or cosmic cycle have no established observational support.

Research protocol

Distinguish direct observation, inference, simulation and speculation. Record mass uncertainties, observation epoch, proposed formation mechanism and testable electromagnetic, neutrino or gravitational-wave signatures. A mass threshold is not a formation history.
